# The County of Bruma

The seat of County Bruma, kept on the web: its watch, its holdings, its purse,
its guilds, its court and its archive. Node and Express, flat JSON files, no database.

## Running it on Railway

Add it as a second service in the same project as the Ministry Portal
(**Add New Service -> GitHub Repo -> WosyJr/bruma**). Each service keeps its own
domain, its own variables and its own volume.

### Variables

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| `SESSION_SECRET` | A long random string. Changing it signs everybody out. |
| `IDLE_MINUTES` | How long someone may sit idle before the hall closes on them. Default 30. |
| `AUDIT_KEY` | The same long random string as on the Ministry. Its **Every Hand** page then reads this site's sign-ins, strikes and doings from `/audit.json`. |
| `MASTER_USERNAME` | The name the first account enters with, e.g. `wosy` |
| `MASTER_PASSWORD` | Its first word. Change it the moment you are in. |
| `MASTER_NAME` | The name shown on screen, e.g. `Wosy` |
| `DATA_DIR` | `/data` — must match the volume's mount path |
| `NODE_ENV` | `production` |
| `CURRENT_YEAR` | The year of the Fourth Era the County is in. Default 226. |
| `GHOST_ODDS` | How rare the pale figure is: one page in this many. Default 200. |
| `DISCORD_WEBHOOK` | Optional. A Discord webhook address for the public news channel (channel settings, Integrations, Webhooks, New Webhook, Copy Webhook URL). |
| `DISCORD_OFFICERS_WEBHOOK` | Optional. A second webhook, for a channel only officers can see. |
| `DISCORD_WEBHOOK_A` … `DISCORD_WEBHOOK_F` | Optional. Up to six more channels. None of the webhook variables is needed any more: every channel's address can be pasted on `/discord` instead, where it is kept in `discord.json` on the volume and shown only masked. An address set on the site wins over the Railway variable for that slot. |
| `SITE_URL` | Optional. The site's own address, e.g. `https://bruma.up.railway.app`, so Discord posts link back and carry portraits. Learned from the first visit if not set. |
| `TIMEZONE` | The clock the County keeps: the day on the banner, the ballad's one line a day, the Face of the Day and the Herald's week all turn over at midnight here. Default `America/New_York`. |

### The volume

Attach a volume mounted at **`/data`**. Everything the County records lives there:
people, offices, shifts, holdings, the ledger, guild rolls, court matters and the archive.
Without it, a redeploy wipes the County clean.

The first account is made only when `people.json` does not yet exist. After that the
variables are ignored, and accounts are made in **The Officers**.

## The offices

Three offices **hold the County** — they reach every hall and every power, including
anything added later:

- **Count of Bruma**
- **Countess of Bruma**
- **Steward of Bruma**

A fourth, **Master of the Hold**, also holds the County and cannot be struck or stripped.
That is the account the variables above create.

Below them: Captain of the Watch, Guardsman, the three Guild Masters, Clerk of the County
and Citizen. Offices are not fixed in the code — make, amend or strike them in
**The Officers -> The offices**, ticking exactly what each one opens.

## The halls

| Hall | What it does |
|---|---|
| **The Watch** | Guards clock on and off at a named post. Every shift is logged with its hours; the Captain keeps the roster, amends the log and writes the day book. |
| **The Property Roll** | The county map with every holding pinned on it. Click the map to drop a pin, set who holds it, what rent it renders, and record rents as they come in. |
| **Guild member logins** | When a Master of a guild (or a member of rank VI.E or above) admits someone, the hall makes a login for them, named after their first name and the guild (`kaelen.miners`), with a word shown once on the hall page to hand over in game; they set their own word the first time they enter. A member sees the public site and their own hall only: the charter, the ladder, the duties, the open contracts and every pin on the hall's map, but never the guild's money. Ranks VI.A to VI.C only look. VI.D (Foreman, Ranger) may also set pins and post contracts, and sees the roll. VI.E and VI.F (Minemaster, Huntmaster, Steward) may also admit, promote up to their own rank, strike, give a member a login and give them a new word. Striking a member or setting them expelled stops their login the same hour; erasing them removes it. On an officer's page the Steward ticks which other guild halls that officer may look into: they see those halls whole but cannot change anything there. For the ten-rank ladders (Fighters, Synod) the same powers sit with the top three and top two ranks. |
| **Live pages** | Every page without its own live feed checks every four seconds whether anything has changed and, if it has, redraws itself in place: the scroll stays where it is, open drop-downs stay open, and nothing is redrawn while you are typing in a form or have just clicked. Forms on those pages send in place too: the page updates under you, the message slides up at the bottom, and only a move to another page reloads. |
| **The Treasury** | An append-only ledger in septims. Nothing is ever edited or deleted — a mistake is put right by a counter-entry, so the books always audit. Views by month and by head. Two accounts, **Checking** (the Coffers) and **Savings** (the Strongbox): every entry says which, *Move money* shifts coin between them as one numbered entry, and the ledger filters by account. **Owed to the County**: every Sundas (Eastern time, checked every 15 minutes so a sleeping server catches up) the rent written on each deed in force, the fee of any licence running out that week, and every tax still owing are added as pending; they do not touch the balance until marked *Collected* (into Checking, under Rents, Trade and tolls or Taxes, and on the holding's rent book or the tax roll) or *Waived* with a reason. Unpaid sums from earlier weeks are marked overdue. *Reckon now* runs it by hand without doubling anything. Rent rendered on a holding's page also enters the treasury and clears what that deed owes. **Where the money comes from** splits money received into rents, licences, taxes, exports, imports and everything else, this week, this month and all time, with what is still owed and the total. *Reset the treasury* (needs the corrections power, type RESET) clears the ledger and both accounts, or everything including what is owed; the old files are kept. |
| **The Guilds** | The Synod, the Miners and the Fighters. Each has its charter, its roll by grade, and its tithes to and from the Treasury. A Guild Master sees only his own. |
| **The Court** | Matters laid before the County, papers entered upon them, hearings set and judgments given. Anyone may lay a matter; only the court may judge. |
| **The Archive** | Every charter, law, deed, order and dispatch, on labelled shelves, searchable, held as text or as a link. |

## What is public, and what is not

Anyone may read these without an account:

**Proclamations** · **The Pale Pass** · **Laws & Charters** (the archive's law and charter shelves only)
· **Who Holds What** (holdings and their holders — never their rents) · **Judgments** (verdicts once given,
never open matters) · **The Court** (offices and who holds them) · **Lay a Petition** (anyone may petition
the County with no account)

Everything else is behind the hall door, and deliberately so. **The watch is never public** — not who is
standing, not the roster, not the log, not the hours. Nor is the Treasury, the guild rolls and tithes, the
matters still before the bench, or the officer admin. What happens in Bruma is found out in Bruma.

An office is named on the public court page only when **Named publicly** is ticked for it in
*The Officers → The offices*. Guardsman and Citizen start unticked, so the watch is not readable as a roster.

## The map

`public/county-map.png`. Pins are stored as a percentage across and down, so swapping
the map for a bigger one keeps every pin where it was.

## Running it locally

    npm install
    MASTER_USERNAME=wosy MASTER_PASSWORD=somethinglong npm start

Then open http://localhost:3000.

## Added lately

- **Who stands where** (`/watch/week`): the watch as a week grid, one row per post, one column per day. Past days show what was stood; today and ahead show who is set. A gap is red with *Take this post*; a stand over sixteen hours gets an amber edge. Postings live in `watch-rota.json`.

## The town

The front page carries **Bruma tonight** and a few things that belong to everyone.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Bruma tonight** | A drawing of the town at night. A window lights for each officer signed in within ten minutes; visitors see the light, officers see whose it is. A wall lamp burns for each guard on watch. What the town has built is drawn in. |
| **The town's goal** (`/goal`) | An office with *Set the town's goals* names a goal and its sum, and may tie it to something drawn in the town: a bell, a beacon, banners, a statue or a new North Gate. Anyone may pledge. A pledge counts once the Steward marks it paid, after the septims change hands in game. When paid reaches the sum, it is built. |
| **The pale figure** | Now and then a figure walks across the page for a few moments. Catch it and your name goes in the Herald. |
| **The ballad** (`/ballad`) | One line a day from anyone. Each week has its own song; past weeks are sealed and kept. An office with the town power strikes a line. |
| **Face of the Day** | The first thing under the County banner: one portrait each day in a gold frame, with the person's line. Anyone can **raise a cup** to them once a day, and the cups go in the Herald. |
| **Faces of Bruma** (`/faces`) | Officers put up their own portrait. Visitors can send one in, and it waits until an office with the town power puts it up or turns it away. Before sending, everyone drags and zooms their picture inside a portrait frame, so it goes up exactly as they placed it. Officers with no portrait get a nudge on their desk, and approvers see portraits waiting. |
| **The Jerall View Inn** (`/inn`) | Thirteen tables played for real septims, open only to patrons the inn's keepers have seated. At the door everyone gives a name and the **passcode** they were told in game; an officer signed in to the hall has their name filled in and types only the passcode. A keeper with the new **inn** power reads the passcode in **The Inn Ledger** (`/inn/ledger`, under The Coffers) and lets them in, one by one or with *Let them all in*. Each new browser waits at the door the same way, even under a seated patron's name (shown as *back from another browser*, with *It's them* / *Not them*), so nobody can play someone else's purse. Keepers fill a purse once coin is handed over in game: *Add*, *Take* or *Set*, each written in the purse book with who did it and a note. Every stake comes out of the purse and every win goes back in; a purse never drops below nothing. The tables: **Twenty-one** (`/inn/cards`), blackjack from a six-deck shoe reshuffled at three quarters, blackjack pays 6 to 5, insurance on an ace, double on any two cards, split pairs up to four hands, surrender, dealer stands on soft 17 (keys H S D P R); **The Wheel of Fortune** (`/inn/wheel`), single-zero roulette with the full cloth: straight, split, street, trio, corner, first four, six line, dozens, columns and the even-money bets; **Hjalmar's Hazard** (`/inn/hazard`), the old dice game: name a main from 5 to 9, nicks and outs, then chase your chance, with a side bet on the exact next total; and **Bones** (`/inn/bones`) played for a stake, first to 50, turns alternating who throws first; **The Bird Cage** (`/inn/cage`), chuck-a-luck: three dice in a turning cage, numbers pay 1, 2 or 3 to 1, high or low even money (losing on three alike), any three alike 30 to 1; **The Jarl's Lottery** (`/inn/keno`), keno for the whole hall: pick one to ten of 80 numbers, twenty balls drop on one shared draw every few minutes (keepers set how often in the ledger and can *Draw the lottery now*), paytables return about 90%, a ticket pays at most a thousand times the table's largest stake, tickets can be torn up before their draw, and the balls drop one by one on every patron's page; and **The Dragon Wheel** (`/inn/six`), a big-six wheel of 54 slots (1 ×24, 2 ×15, 5 ×7, 10 ×4, 20 ×2, Dragon and Septim one each at 40 to 1); **Pull the Lever** (`/inn/slots`), a five-reel, nine-line slot machine with spinning reels: the Dragon is wild, three or more Septims anywhere pay and give eight free spins, and a Dragon on every reel wins the **Dragon's Hoard**, a jackpot that starts at 1,000 and grows by one septim in every hundred staked; **The Barrow** (`/inn/mines`), mines: choose four to twenty-four draugr under twenty-five stones, lift stones for silver at a rising multiplier and take it whenever you like, until a draugr wakes; and **The Avalanche** (`/inn/plinko`), plinko: a snowball drops down sixteen rows of rocks into seventeen slots, from 0.2× to 100× at low, medium or high risk, several in the air at once. Three games are played by the whole hall together, each on its own clock: **The Dragon's Climb** (`/inn/crash`), crash: bet while the flight boards, watch the multiplier climb, cash out by hand or set a height to leave by itself, and lose the stake if you are still riding when it falls (the fall point is drawn before take-off and never sent to a browser until it happens); **The Chorrol Downs** (`/inn/races`), six horses every few minutes (keepers set how often), win, place or show at fixed odds worked out from thousands of simulated races less a tenth, and the race run on every patron's page at once; and **The Arena** (`/inn/arena`), a house bout every few minutes between two of ten fighters, odds from their true chances less a tenth, watched blow by blow with falling health bars. Keepers can also post bouts fought in game from the ledger (*The Arena: bouts fought in game*): two names and the odds on each, betting open until they close it, then *won* for either side pays the backers at once, and *Call it off* hands every stake back. Red or Black and Punto are retired; their past rounds stay in the books, and any Red or Black run left open is handed back at start-up. Bones pays 1.9 times the stake. Side bets: **Perfect Pairs** and **21+3** at Twenty-one and the Long Table, a **named three alike** (180 to 1) at the Bird Cage, **exacta** and **trifecta** at the Chorrol Downs, and **under/over the blows** and **how it ends** (blade, spell or arrow) in the Arena. The Arena is fought on the page: a torch-lit ring where two of thirteen fighters (sword and shield, two-handers, daggers, spears, archers and fire, frost and lightning mages) trade blows, arrows and spells, with damage numbers, blocks, dodges and a knockout, the same fight for everyone. The hall: **tonight's notice** (set by a keeper in the ledger, shown at the door until the night turns), a **crier** scrolling the big wins and tips across the lobby, **tonight's boards** (biggest win, high roller, luckiest), **tip the dealer** from any table (tips go to the house), **happy hour** (a keeper picks a table and a length; wins there pay 10% more of what they won), and **Bard's corner**, a music dock on every inn page that opens a small window of its own (`/inn/bard`) playing the keepers' YouTube list or a patron's own YouTube links (kept in their browser); the window keeps playing while the patron moves between pages, can be sized by dragging its edges, and the first page of each visit points to it. A patron waiting at the door has their page open by itself the moment a keeper lets them in (with a *Check again* button too). At the Avalanche the smallest drop rises with the risk (×2 at medium, ×5 at high), and at the Barrow the smallest stake rises with the draugr (×2 from 10, ×5 from 18). The ledger and *How the tables ran* show the **coin in hand**: coin handed over in game less coin paid out, what is owed to patrons (purses, cash-outs set aside, bets still riding), and the house's own. Keepers can also lean on the tables from the live view, under a folded *House settings* panel whose controls (including the one on each patron's card) only show while it is open: a whole table or one patron, a little, firmly or hard; nothing of it is shown to patrons. Every card, die and spin is drawn on the server with Node's cryptographic random generator; each table shows its last 20 outcomes, and keepers see **How the tables ran** (`/inn/fairness`): what each table kept this week against what its rules should keep. The ledger sets each table's smallest and largest stake, so nobody can empty the house in one bet (largest by default 2,000, or 1,000 at the Lottery, Slots and the Avalanche; side bets are capped at it too). Every table shows the patron what they have won or lost tonight, overall and at that table, updating with each round. It also sets the most the lottery pays on one ticket, a daily loss cap per patron (off by default), whether Brannoc hits soft 17, and whether any two ten-cards split. Patrons see their own purse, how they stand at the tables, their last 50 rounds and purse entries at `/inn/me`. **Cash out** sets the sum aside from the purse at once (so it cannot be played away) until a keeper marks it *Paid in game* or puts it back; **Ask for more coin** sends a request a keeper fills once the coin is handed over. Both appear at the top of the ledger with how much that patron has won or lost at the tables, and post to the officers' Discord channel. **Watch the tables live** (`/inn/ledger/live`) shows everyone who has played in the last fifteen minutes, at the table they are on, as they see it, with a card for each patron that stays in place (in name order) and changes only when that patron moves, a glow on whatever changed, how long ago each patron last moved, a filter by table, a summary of the last fifteen minutes (patrons, rounds, staked, how the house stands, back-room tables), and each round as it finishes. Every table has sounds (cards, chips, dice, the wheel's ticking, wins and losses, all made in the browser, with a switch to turn them off) and animations (dealt cards, rolling dice, the spinning wheel, chips dropping on the cloth, the purse counting up or down, a glow on a win and a burst of coins on a big one); people who ask their system for less motion get the sounds without the movement. Five hands running at Twenty-one is *Luck of the Nine*: it posts to Discord, is listed in the ledger, and is on the Roll of Honour to give to officers. The Herald carries the week's biggest win, biggest loss and how the house ended. **Where tonight**: the inn moves every night (the night turns at noon, Eastern time) through fourteen hiding places, picked from a secret seed so nobody can work out the next one, and never the same place two nights running; the door and the lobby show tonight's place, and the ledger shows tonight's and tomorrow's and lets a keeper move it for tonight by hand. **The back room** (`/inn/back`) is patron against patron: **Bones Duel** (two players each put down a stake, same bones and frost, first to 50 takes both), **Bruma Bluff** (liar's dice for two to six: five dice under each cup, ones wild, bid higher or call liar, last with dice takes the pot) and **Hold'em** (Texas hold'em for two to six with a buy-in, blinds, minimum raises, side pots for all-ins, the uncalled bet returned, and a cash-in whenever you leave), **The Long Table** (blackjack against Brannoc for up to five, everyone's cards face up: a twenty-second betting window each round, insurance, peek, split to four, double, surrender, seat-by-seat turns; A seated patron opens a table and others sit down; stakes leave the purse on sitting down and the winner's share goes straight back. The house keeps only its cut (5% by default, set in the ledger; at Hold'em only from pots that see a flop, at most three big blinds). Every turn has a timer (45 seconds by default, set in the ledger): when it runs out the duel banks or passes, the bluff table bids or calls, and Hold'em checks or folds; three missed turns forfeit a duel or put a bluffer out, and a Hold'em player who misses two sits out and is cashed in after five minutes away. Tables update by themselves for everyone at them, with sounds, table talk and a turn bar; you see only your own dice and cards. Keepers watch every back-room table live with every hand shown, and the rounds count in the ledger, *How the tables ran* and each patron's record. ## The top bar, the town's doors and the gate

Officers' pages have a grouped bar: *The Great Hall* and *The Watch* stay as plain links, and the rest sit under *The Coffers* (treasury, tax roll, licences, property), *Law* (court, gaol, reckoner, petitions), *The People* (people, guilds, service, officers) and *The County* (proclaim, archive, announcements, flourishes), each a menu with a one-line hint. *The Public Face* stays at the right. Visitors keep the flat public bar.

Under the Pale Pass line on every page, a row of six doors leads to the Herald, the ballad, the faces, the inn, the honours and the goal. An amber dot on a door means something is new there since that browser last looked.

On the Proclaim page, *Cry it at the gate* (ticked by default) makes the proclamation meet everyone who next opens the site, as a writ over the page. *I have read it* puts it away for good (remembered on the login for officers, in the browser for visitors, in `gate-seen.json`); *Later* keeps it away until tomorrow. Never more than one a visit; older unread ones wait their turn.

## Discord announcements

`/discord` (the new power *Choose what the County announces on Discord*) shows one card per channel (up to eight). On each the Steward pastes the channel's webhook address (or leaves it to Railway), names it, and sends a test to see where it lands; when an address is saved the site asks Discord what the webhook is called and shows that too. The Steward then tick which of 22 kinds of post run and choose the channel each kind goes to, previews each one exactly as Discord will show it, posts a test or a sample on demand, and lists what was lately sent. Any kind of post can also have a webhook of its own: pick *Its own channel…* in its drop-down, paste the address, save. Only that kind goes there, and it ignores the channel cards. The Herald goes out as a picture of the whole paper (`/herald.png`, drawn on the server with `@napi-rs/canvas` and the fonts in `vendor/fonts/`), attached to the post; if the picture cannot be drawn the stories go as text instead.

Every post carries its own banner from `public/discord/` (a 1200×300 painting for each kind, with the County's flag hanging on it), attached to the post itself so Discord never has to fetch it from the site. When a webhook address is saved on the site, the site also sets the County seal as that webhook's own face in Discord (only if the webhook had no face yet). Posts that fail are listed under *Lately sent* with Discord's reason, kept in `discord-log.json` across restarts, and counted in a red notice at the top of the page; proclamations, judgments and honours quote their text in a block, names are in bold, and each post ends with an arrow link back to the page it came from.

Sent to the public channel unless moved: proclamations, the Pale Pass changing state, judgments, the gaol (names and writ numbers only, never the grounds), new laws, town goals set and built, the pale figure caught, honours, oaths and raises, Lantern of the Watch milestones, Luck of the Nine (five in a row at Twenty-one), and on a schedule (Eastern time) the Face of the Day at 8 each morning, the Herald and the inn's champions on Sundas at 8 in the evening, and last week's ballad just after midnight on Morndas.

Sent to the officers' channel unless moved: new petitions, sent-in portraits waiting, someone asking for a seat at the inn (with the passcode they gave), cash-outs and requests for more coin, the treasury going overdrawn, what is owed each Sundas, and each morning at 9 any guard who was on the rota yesterday and never clocked on.

Posts can never ping anyone (`allowed_mentions` is empty), they queue and wait out Discord's rate limits, and a failure never stops anything on the site. Without the webhook variables, nothing is sent.
